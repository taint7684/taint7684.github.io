Battery 2030: preparing for Vietnam's first wave of end-of-life EV batteries

Lithium batteries from electric motorbikes start reaching end of life in volume around 2029–2030 — the same year Vietnam's recycling obligations are due for revision. What should a manufacturer build, and when?

Recommendation in one sentence

Run the lead-acid battery take-back in-house now, build the battery identification platform from 2027, and choose the EPR option for lithium batteries before 30 June 2028 using measured second-life prices.

1. Start from the regulation

I extracted obligations verbatim from Vietnam's EPR decree and environmental law, and from the EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542 as a reference standard, then compared them across 18 topics.

LevelTopicsMeaning
⛔ Critical gap6Vietnam has no equivalent rule, so recovery and second life cannot be measured. The platform sets the requirement voluntarily, to EU standard.
⚠️ Partial7A rule exists but is weak or vague. Meet Vietnam's rule, extend data fields to EU level.
✏️ Minor5Wording differences or out of scope. Recorded, no separate requirement.

Three findings that shaped the design

  • Lithium EV batteries currently carry a 0% recycling rate, so the fund contribution is also zero. The earliest revision is 2029; a draft proposes 8% from 2029 and 15% from 2032.
  • Lead-acid batteries have a 12% obligation now. The fictitious client must register a plan or pay into the fund for responsibility year 2026, due before 1 April 2027.
  • Vietnam measures recycled mass; the EU measures collection rate. No collection target, no second-life rule and no battery ID in Vietnam — which is exactly why the platform needs to exist.

2. Forecast the wave

A cohort × Weibull model turns yearly sales into end-of-life volumes, with 1,000 Monte Carlo runs per scenario to show a range rather than a single number.

  • Lithium end-of-life volume passes 10 kt/year in 2029–2030 and 30 kt/year in 2031–2034.
  • Lead-acid is today's problem: ≈33 kt/year in 2030, larger than lithium until 2033.
  • About 45% of batteries are technically fit for a second life, but only ≈16% reach it in 2030, because most flow through undocumented channels. Second life is blocked by collection, not by technology.
  • Changing battery lifetime by ±2 years shifts the threshold year by about one year. The forecast matches public filings and an independent estimate within the checks applied.

3. Design the platform

Eight modules: battery register, collection, state-of-health (SoH) assessment, routing to second life or recycling, partners, EPR reporting, battery passport, and administration. The MVP covers 25 user stories in five sprints, in time for the April 2027 filing deadline.

Deliverables

  • Charter, BACCM, PESTLE/SWOT, stakeholders and RACI, personas, as-is BPMN and root cause
  • Vision & scope, BPMN to-be, SRS (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148): 33 FR, 11 NFR, 30 business rules, 8 AI requirements
  • Decision tables, ERD, OpenAPI, story map and backlog
  • Test plan, 73 automated test cases, traceability matrix and UAT

4. AI with guardrails

A model estimates battery state of health from public NASA battery data. It is accurate on average — mean absolute error 2.53 points, 88.5% interval coverage — but not yet safe to decide alone: a business rule sends 94.6% of cases to manual measurement because the interval is too wide.

Decision

The MVP runs the model in parallel with manual testing. Automatic decisions switch on only after the model meets its acceptance requirements on the client's own battery-pack data.

5. Business case: build, pay into the fund, or outsource?

Three options were compared for 2027–2035, separately for lead-acid and lithium, with 2,000 Monte Carlo runs. All money figures rest on low-confidence assumptions and are shown as ranges.

The NPV range for lithium is wide and changes sign, so the decision depends on the measured second-life price. That is why the recommendation sets a deadline to decide, rather than deciding now.

What I learned

  • A 0% obligation is still a requirement. The rate is scheduled for revision, so the platform stores rates and fees as effective-dated parameters instead of hard-coding today's values.
  • Present forecasts as ranges. Battery lifetime moves the answer more than sales do; showing P10–P90 made that visible to decision makers.
  • Specify how AI is allowed to fail. Writing acceptance requirements for the model turned "AI estimates SoH" into a testable rule about when a human must take over.
