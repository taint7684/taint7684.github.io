The EUDR problem in Vietnamese coffee is a data problem

What does a mid-size Vietnamese coffee exporter need to build to keep selling to the EU under the Deforestation Regulation — and is it worth it?

Three findings
  • Missing data is the binding constraint, not deforestation. Without plot coordinates no due diligence statement (DDS) can be filed, which puts USD 245–472 m/year of Đắk Lắk coffee exports to the EU at risk.
  • Vietnam is low-risk under EUDR, but plot-level evidence is still required. The exporter is not the EUDR operator, so the system produces a DDS data package for the EU importer.
  • Tree cover loss is not deforestation. In core coffee districts the loss is almost entirely agricultural — most likely replanting, not forest conversion.

1. Start from the law, not from a summary

Most EUDR content online is secondary commentary. I worked from the official texts: the 2023 regulation, the December 2025 amendment that postponed and simplified it, and the 2025 country benchmarking act. I extracted 40 obligations into a requirements matrix.

The verbatim column of that matrix is filled by a script that searches the official text for an anchor phrase and stops if the phrase is missing or ambiguous. No legal quote is typed by hand.

Reading the primary text changed the design three times

FindingSourceDesign impact
Vietnam is classified low-riskBenchmarking act, Art. 13No formal risk mitigation, but every plot still needs geolocation and verifiable evidence (Art. 9).
The exporter is not the "operator"Art. 7My first idea — "the system submits the DDS to the EU" — was legally wrong. The system builds a DDS data package for the importer.
Agroforestry is not forestArt. 2(6)Coffee under shade trees must not be flagged as deforestation by satellite rules.

The gap analysis scored 14 obligations as not met, 12 as partly met and 14 as needing interpretation. The largest gaps: no plot coordinates, mixing across four or five supply-chain tiers, no verifiable deforestation-free evidence, undigitised land-use documents and no data channel to the EU importer.

2. Measure risk with public satellite data, then distrust it

Global Forest Watch publishes tree-cover loss per district with its dominant driver. For Đắk Lắk (15 districts, pre-2025 boundaries):

  • 15,873 ha of tree cover were lost in 2021–2025, 48% less than in 2016–2020.
  • Five districts account for 80% of that loss.
  • In the core coffee district Cư M'gar, 99.9% of the loss is classified as agricultural.

A naive reading flags Cư M'gar as a deforestation hotspot. "Tree cover loss" counts every tree over five metres, including shade trees and old coffee being replanted — which is not deforestation under EUDR. I therefore labelled the district index a screening indicator and moved the real decision to plot level.

At plot level the system overlays each plot on the EU's 2020 forest map and post-2020 loss, then applies a six-rule decision table. When sources disagree, the plot goes to a gray zone and cannot enter a DDS package until it is verified.

3. Design the system so the trace is checkable

The solution, ECTS, has five capability groups: plot registration, plot risk screening, purchase recording, DDS data package, and dashboard/audit.

Deliverables

  • BPMN to-be process and RACI
  • SRS following ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148: 24 functional requirements, 28 business rules in EARS, 12 NFRs
  • 31 user stories with 58 Gherkin acceptance criteria; story map, DoR/DoD, release plan
  • Screen specs, state model, ERD, sequence diagrams and permissions
  • 88 test cases from decision tables, boundary values and equivalence classes
  • Clickable prototype running on 298 synthetic plots
Traceability that cannot drift

Requirements, stories, criteria and tests live in CSV files. A build script generates the traceability matrix and fails if any unmet EUDR obligation does not reach a test case. The same script fills the tables in the SRS. Result: 14 of 14 unmet obligations traced to tests, zero errors.

4. Business case: data is the binding constraint

For a fictitious exporter (30,000 t/year, 45% to the EU) I compared three options over three years.

A. BuildB. Rent SaaSC. Consortium
3-year NPV, likely (USD m)3.464.434.04
Weighted score, exporter / farmer view2.95 / 3.153.65 / 3.154.30 / 4.50

Recommendation: rent SaaS now — the only option that can go live before the deadline — with a contractual data-export clause, then migrate to an industry consortium where farmers register plots once and share them across buyers.

Sensitivity analysis showed the three parameters that matter all sit on the benefit side: price penalty avoided, EU share and volume. The analysis also states when not to invest: below a price penalty of about 1.2–1.6%, or an EU share below 3–5%, no option pays back.

What I learned

  • Read the regulation itself. Secondary summaries missed the amendment, the low-risk classification and the operator definition — each of which changed the design.
  • Talk to the customer's customer early. The weakest assumption is the market price penalty; one conversation with an EU importer would tighten the model more than any calculation.
  • Get official statistics before ranking risk. The district with the most tree-cover loss grows little coffee; using the statistical yearbook moved the coffee heartland to the top of the list.
